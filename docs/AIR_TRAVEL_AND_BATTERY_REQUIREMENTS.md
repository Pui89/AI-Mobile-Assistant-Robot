# PUI89 AI NOMAD — Air Travel & Battery Requirements

**Purpose:** engineering checklist for a suitcase-form smart device that may be carried by air.  
**Status:** design requirements only; not an airline approval, certification or legal interpretation.

## 1. Important distinction

“Looks like a carry-on suitcase” and “accepted as cabin baggage” are different claims. Airline size, weight, baggage class, battery and smart-bag policies vary by carrier, route, cabin class and aircraft. NOMAD's proposed 8–10 kg complete mass (12 kg ceiling) may exceed some cabin baggage weight limits.

A commonly referenced IATA cabin-baggage dimension example is 56 × 45 × 25 cm, including wheels and handles, but this is not a universal allowance. NOMAD's package target is approximately 30–33 × 35–40 × 35–40 cm; final fit must be checked in all dimensions and against the operating airline's rules.

## 2. Design requirements

- Removable battery pack with documented chemistry, rated voltage, capacity and Wh.
- Battery protection/BMS, over-current and short-circuit protection, thermal monitoring, isolation and secure mechanical retention.
- Battery terminals protected during removal and transport.
- Tool-light or tool-free removal that does not expose live conductors or require opening hazardous electrical compartments.
- Clear product label and user instructions showing battery Wh and removal procedure.
- Flight mode that fully powers down the robot and disables drive electronics.
- No charging, powered wheel movement or autonomous operation onboard.
- If the bag is gate-checked, owner can remove the battery and carry it separately as required.
- Mechanical design must protect the battery against crush, puncture, impact, water ingress and accidental activation.

## 3. Airline review checklist

Before each flight, verify with every operating carrier:
1. Cabin or checked baggage size and weight limits for the exact route and aircraft.
2. Whether powered smart luggage or self-propelled baggage is accepted.
3. Whether the battery must be removed for cabin carriage or checked baggage.
4. Battery Wh thresholds, operator approval and spare-battery limits.
5. Whether the product's radios, tracking or wireless functions are restricted.
6. Security-screening and inspection requirements.
7. Transit/connection carrier differences.

Do not rely on a single airline's policy for a multi-carrier itinerary.

## 4. Evidence needed before any travel claim

- Measured product dimensions with calibrated measuring equipment; include wheels, handle recesses, corner guards and protrusions.
- Measured complete mass and battery mass.
- Battery datasheet and permanent Wh marking.
- BMS and electrical safety evidence.
- Safe removal/terminal-protection procedure.
- Written carrier response or documented acceptance for a specific product configuration, route and cabin class.
- Date of policy check and operating carrier identity.

## 5. Non-claims

Do not say “IATA approved”, “airline approved”, “fits every overhead bin”, “universally cabin compatible” or “allowed on all flights”. IATA guidance explains general baggage and battery principles; the airline's current conditions and applicable regulations govern actual carriage.
